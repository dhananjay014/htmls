Generative recommendation · ICML 2024

Actions become the sequence.

HSTU is easier to understand once you separate two ideas: a recommender can be reformulated as a sequence model, and that sequence model needs a block designed for long, sparse, time-stamped behavior histories.

First separate the three levels of the paper

Level 1 · problem formulationGenerative Recommender

Turn recommendation into sequence-to-sequence prediction: next content for retrieval, or next response after inserting a candidate for ranking.

Level 2 · neural operatorHSTU encoder

A specialized residual block with fused U/V/Q/K projection, pointwise SiLU attention, temporal bias, LayerNorm, and an elementwise gate.

Level 3 · systems algorithmsMake scale usable

Ragged kernels, Stochastic Length, activation fusion, embedding placement, and M-FALCON reduce training and serving cost.

Vocabulary discipline. GR is the task/data/training paradigm. HSTU is one encoder designed for it. M-FALCON is a candidate-serving algorithm that can also apply to other causal target-aware attention models.

The problem in one picture

1 · Reframe

Replace many hand-engineered snapshots with a chronological stream of high-cardinality categorical events.

2 · Transduce

Predict the next content token for retrieval or the response token attached to a candidate for ranking.

3 · Scale

Use an attention block, sparse-length training, fused kernels, and amortized candidate scoring designed together.

Four facts to keep separate

HSTU is not merely “a faster Transformer.”

Its pointwise attention deliberately removes sequence-wise softmax normalization, and its output uses an elementwise gate instead of a standard Transformer feed-forward sublayer.

Generative does not only mean text generation.

The output vocabulary can contain atomic item and action IDs. The model generates recommendations by modeling a probability distribution over those tokens.

Ranking and retrieval use different targets.

Retrieval predicts which content comes next. Ranking inserts a target item into the causal stream and predicts the action a user would take on it.

The trillion parameters are mostly embeddings.

Large, non-stationary ID vocabularies make embedding storage central. The paper separately studies scaling of embedding and non-embedding capacity.

Memory hook

Sequence the actions → aggregate history without softmax → gate the result → amortize the expensive work across targets.

Step 1 · Change the representation

From feature snapshots to sequential transduction.

A traditional DLRM sees a constructed feature vector for one impression. A Generative Recommender sees the evolving history itself and reuses one causal encoding for many supervised positions.

Why training becomes generative

What happens to other feature types?

Fast categorical events

Items and actions naturally become the main token stream.

Slow categorical state

Demographics or followed creators are compressed into change points, then merged into the timeline.

Dense aggregates

The proposal removes many counters and asks target-aware sequence modeling to reconstruct their useful signal. This is an approximation, not an identity.

Do not overstate the equivalence.

A finite sequence does not literally contain every engineered feature. The paper argues that the unified sequence can approximate the full feature space as history length and model expressiveness grow.

Step 2 · Learn the operator

Inside one HSTU layer.

One fused projection creates queries, keys, values, and a gate. Pointwise attention pools the history; LayerNorm controls scale; the gate and output projection transform the result.

Q · “What am I looking for?”

One query vector per current token and head.

K · “What do I match?”

Historical coordinates compared with Q.

V · “What content do I send?”

Historical channels aggregated by pointwise weights.

U · “Which channels pass now?”

Current-token gate multiplied after pooled-history normalization.

The three equations

Forward pass for \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\)

1
\[U,V,Q,K=\operatorname{Split}(\operatorname{SiLU}(XW_1+b_1))\]
Pointwise projection.One fused linear map makes all four tensors. \(Q,K\) determine relevance, \(V\) carries content, and \(U\) is an input-dependent gate.
2
\[A=\operatorname{SiLU}(QK^\top+R_{p,t}),\qquad P=AV\]
Spatial aggregation.Pairwise scores receive learned relative position/time bias. SiLU acts independently on each score—there is no row-wise softmax denominator.
3
\[Y(X)=f_2\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(P)\odot U\right),\qquad X'=X+Y(X)\]
Gated transformation.LayerNorm stabilizes the unnormalized pool. The elementwise product lets the current token decide which pooled channels pass through.
X: N × dN sequence positions, width d.
Q,K: h × N × d_qkPer-head queries and keys.
U,V: h × N × d_vPer-head gates and values.
A: h × N × NCausal attention weights.
R_p,t: 1 × N × NShared positional + temporal bias.
W₂: (h d_v) × dReturns concatenated heads to width d.

Why this can replace several DLRM modules

Extraction

Attention pools relevant historical ID embeddings instead of fetching many pre-aggregated feature tables.

Interaction

The dot product forms a target-history interaction; \(\operatorname{LN}(AV)\odot U\) directly crosses pooled context with the current token.

Transformation

The input-dependent gate behaves like conditional routing across channels, replacing a separate wide feed-forward sublayer.

SwiGLU connection. Because SiLU is applied to \(U\), the product \(\operatorname{LN}(AV)\odot U\) resembles a SwiGLU-style gated transform. The important difference is that one branch is an attention-pooled historical representation.
Step 3 · Understand the key difference

Pointwise attention keeps intensity.

Softmax answers “how should one unit of attention be divided?” HSTU’s pointwise activation can also represent “how much matching evidence exists?”

Transformer-style row normalization\[a_{ij}^{\text{soft}}=\frac{e^{s_{ij}}}{\sum_k e^{s_{ik}}},\quad \sum_j a_{ij}^{\text{soft}}=1\]

Relative allocation is explicit; total mass is fixed.

HSTU pointwise activation\[a_{ij}^{\text{HSTU}}=\operatorname{SiLU}(s_{ij}),\quad s_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j+r_{ij}\]

Each edge is transformed independently; total mass can vary.

Why recommendation is unusual

Strength and frequency can matter.

Ten related long views can indicate stronger preference than one. Softmax must divide one unit of mass among them; pointwise weights can accumulate before normalization.

The vocabulary keeps changing.

New content and actions arrive in a streaming system. The paper reports that pointwise attention is better suited to its synthetic non-stationary-vocabulary setup.

Time gaps are not token gaps.

Relative bias includes both position and elapsed time, so two adjacent actions separated by a month need not look like two adjacent actions separated by seconds.

LayerNorm becomes essential.

Without a normalized attention simplex, pooled magnitude can drift with history and logits. The post-pooling LayerNorm controls this scale.

What the mechanism does not guarantee

Negative SiLU weightsFor negative logits, SiLU is negative. The pool is a signed interaction, not a probability-weighted average.
No automatic noise robustnessSoftmax’s competition can suppress weak edges. Pointwise aggregation may accumulate irrelevant signal if representations and masks are poor.
Still quadratic before sparsityThe full \(QK^\top\) interaction has \(N^2\) pair structure. Kernel fusion changes wall time and memory traffic, not the dense mathematical graph.
Attention is not explanationA large weight is only one factor; values, gates, residuals, later layers, and task heads all affect the prediction.

Check your intuition

If the same relevant event is duplicated, which attention form can naturally increase pre-normalization pooled magnitude?

Pointwise attention. Softmax renormalizes the row to sum to one, so duplication redistributes a fixed mass. HSTU applies SiLU edge by edge and can accumulate both copies, though LayerNorm later controls representation scale.
Step 4 · Make long histories affordable

The architecture and the system are one design.

HSTU’s quality story depends on long sequences. Its engineering story is therefore not optional: ragged kernels, stochastic length, memory reduction, embedding sharding, and candidate-cost amortization are part of the proposal.

Four scaling levers

Ragged attention

Operate on each user’s true history length instead of padding every sample to the maximum.

Stochastic Length

Subsample very long, repetitive histories during training to reduce expected pair cost.

Fused activation path

Fuse projection, nonlinearity, normalization, dropout, and output work to reduce stored intermediates.

M-FALCON

Microbatch candidates so expensive history computation is shared during target-aware ranking.

Stochastic Length in one equation

Paper’s asymptotic result\[\text{dense attention cost } O(N^2d)\quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{expected SL cost }O(N^{\alpha}d),\qquad 1<\alpha\le 2\]

For users below a length threshold, keep the full history. For longer users, sample a shorter subsequence with a probability chosen so the expected cost follows the desired exponent. Lower \(\alpha\) means more aggressive sparsification. It is a training-time approximation and must be validated for quality.

Serving many candidate targets

M-FALCON appends a microbatch of \(b_m\) candidates to one history. A modified mask lets every candidate read the history and itself, while forbidding candidate \(c_i\) from reading any other candidate \(c_j\). The final \(b_m\) states are therefore identical to \(b_m\) separate target-aware passes, but history work is shared.

1 · Batch candidates

One first pass over \(n+b_m\) tokens replaces \(b_m\) passes over \(n+1\) tokens.

2 · Cache history K/V

Across later microbatches, reuse every layer’s history keys and values instead of re-encoding them.

3 · Compute candidate rows

Only candidate U/Q/K/V and candidate-to-history interactions are new for each cached pass.

Separate target-aware passes\[O(mn^2d)\]

Repeat the whole length-\(n\) encoder for every candidate.

One microbatch\[O((n+b_m)^2d)\approx O(n^2d)\quad\text{when }b_m=O(n)\]

Share history work across \(b_m\) candidates.

Later cached microbatch\[O(b_md^2+b_mnd)\]

Paper’s complexity after reusing per-layer history K/V.

Read “encode once” carefully.

The first microbatch builds cacheable per-layer history K/V states. Later microbatches still run candidate projections, candidate-to-history attention, gates, and output transforms; they do not get candidate scores for free.

Evidence scope. The paper reports serving a model described as 285× more complex than its DLRM source while achieving 1.50×/2.99× higher QPS at 1,024/16,384 candidates in tested configurations. This is a system-specific result, not a universal multiplier.

Embedding scale is its own bottleneck

With a 10-billion-entry vocabulary and 512-dimensional embeddings, the paper estimates roughly 60 TB for fp32 embeddings plus Adam states. It uses row-wise AdamW and places optimizer state in DRAM to reduce HBM pressure. HSTU does not make the embedding table disappear; the system shards and manages it.

5 · Gate with U

\[ z=\operatorname{LN}(P)\odot U=[1,-1]\odot[0.5,2]=[0.5,-2]. \]

The current candidate allows half of the first normalized channel through and doubles the magnitude of the second channel. This is the target-conditioned feature interaction that replaces a separate wide FFN.

6 · Output projection and residual

\[ W_2=\begin{bmatrix}1&0.5\\-0.5&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad Y=zW_2=[1.5,-1.75] \] \[ x'=x+Y=[2,1]+[1.5,-1.75]=[3.5,-0.75]. \]
The whole block in words

The candidate query prefers event B; pointwise SiLU pools both event values with unconstrained mass; LayerNorm stabilizes the two-channel pool; U decides which normalized channels pass; \(W_2\) mixes them back to model width; the residual preserves a direct route from \(x\).

Where time enters

If event B is very old, its learned time bucket could contribute \(r_2=-1\), changing its score from 2 to 1 before SiLU. Position and elapsed time alter the interaction weight, not the value vector directly:

Relative position + time\[s_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j+r_{\text{position}(i-j),\,\text{time}(t_i-t_j)}\]

Implementation trap

Can you replace pointwise SiLU with softmax and still call the operator unchanged HSTU?

No. It remains an attention model and may be a useful ablation, but it removes HSTU’s pointwise aggregated attention. The paper reports this replacement as an ablated HSTU.
Read claims with the right denominator

What the paper shows—and what it does not.

HSTU combines public benchmark results, controlled encoder comparisons, and proprietary production experiments. Their evidentiary scopes are different.

Reported results

up to 65.8%NDCG improvement

Reported on the Amazon Books public sequential recommendation setup versus the listed baseline.

5.3×–15.2×encoder speedup

Reported against FlashAttention2-based Transformers at sequence length 8192 in tested training/inference settings.

12.4%production metric improvement

Aggregate paper claim across deployed HSTU-based Generative Recommenders on a large platform.

Scaling claim

Empirical fit, retrieval example\[\text{HitRate@100}\approx 0.15+0.0195\ln C\]

The paper reports power-law-like metric trends with training compute across roughly three orders of magnitude, up to configurations with 8192-token histories, width 1024, 24 HSTU layers, and 1.5-trillion-parameter models. It also reports DLRM quality flattening earlier in the tested internal regime.

A fitted trend is not an architectural theorem.

The curve depends on one company’s data distribution, objectives, model allocation, optimizer, streaming setup, and compute range. It does not prove every HSTU deployment will scale, nor that sequence length alone causes the gain.

Interview drill

Why remove softmax?

To retain absolute evidence intensity and improve behavior under a large, non-stationary vocabulary; LayerNorm then stabilizes pooled scale.

Why is \(U\) needed?

It gates the normalized history pool using the current token’s projected channels, creating a conditional feature interaction and replacing a large FFN path.

Where is target awareness?

For ranking, the candidate is inserted before the action target, so its query can attend causally to the whole prior behavior history.

What remains quadratic?

The pairwise attention graph in sequence length. Ragged kernels and Stochastic Length reduce realized work but do not turn dense attention into a linear operator.

HSTU vs GR?

GR is the recommendation formulation and training paradigm; HSTU is its specialized sequential encoder architecture.

Biggest caveat?

The strongest scaling and production evidence is proprietary and system-dependent, so transfer must be re-measured.
