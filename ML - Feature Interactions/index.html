A visual field guide

How models learn that features matter together.

A click is rarely caused by one feature. “Student,” “Friday night,” and “food delivery” become predictive as a combination. Feature-interaction models differ mainly in which combinations they can express, how explicitly they construct them, and what computation they spend.

Interaction means non-additivity.

If the effect of “running shoes” changes depending on age, the joint effect cannot be reproduced by adding an age score and a category score. Mathematically, the prediction contains a term involving both variables, such as \(w_{ij}x_ix_j\).

Four recurring design ideas

Memorize a named cross

A wide linear model places a weight directly on a chosen conjunction. Precise and interpretable, but somebody must choose the cross.

Factorize pairwise weights

FM represents \(w_{ij}\) as an embedding dot product, sharing statistical strength across sparse pairs.

Compose interactions through depth

MLPs, cross layers, attention, CINs, and stacked FMs repeatedly transform representations to reach higher orders.

Ensemble interaction operators

DHEN accepts that no single operator wins everywhere and mixes heterogeneous modules in a hierarchy.

Use these distinctions while reading

Explicit

The architecture exposes a known algebraic interaction form or bounded order.

Implicit

An MLP may represent interactions, but they are distributed across nonlinear hidden units.

Field-wise

Operations preserve field embeddings rather than flattening every embedding coordinate immediately.

From raw records to interaction tensors

Categorical IDs are normally not fed to these models as integers: ID 900 is not “larger” than ID 12. Each active ID selects a row from its field’s embedding table. A record with \(m\) fields therefore becomes \(E\in\mathbb R^{m\times k}\). FM and AutoInt operate on that field axis; DCN typically flattens it into \(x_0\in\mathbb R^{mk}\); DeepFM sends it down both paths.

Three meanings of “order” that are easy to confuse

  • Algebraic order: the number of raw variables multiplied in a monomial, such as \(x_1x_3x_8\), which is third order.
  • Network depth: the number of transformation layers. Depth may increase reachable order, but the relation depends on the operator.
  • Embedding dimension: latent rank/capacity \(k\). A 64-dimensional FM is still second order; dimension does not mean interaction order.
Leakage caveatA perfect cross may encode future information. Timestamp every feature and construct it as of prediction time.
Rare-cross caveatHigh-order capacity can memorize noise. Minimum-frequency handling, hashing, regularization, and held-out time splits matter.
Metric caveatAUC measures ranking, log loss calibration, and normalized entropy relative uncertainty. They answer different questions.
Serving caveatEmbedding lookup and network compute are only part of latency; feature retrieval and cross consistency often dominate.

Check your intuition

Why does a plain linear model miss “mobile users click this item only in the morning”?

Its logit is additive: \(w_1x_{mobile}+w_2x_{morning}\). It lacks a product/conjunction term \(w_{12}x_{mobile}x_{morning}\) whose contribution exists only when both are active.

Practical selection heuristic

Need a reliable baseline?

Start with FM, then DeepFM. They reveal whether pairwise factorization plus an MLP captures most of the gain.

Need explicit high-order crosses cheaply?

Try DCN v2 with low-rank matrices; compare stacked and parallel arrangements.

Field relations vary by example?

AutoInt gives input-dependent attention over fields. Validate latency at your field count.

Scaling a mature industrial stack?

DHEN or Wukong become relevant once data, compute, training infrastructure, and strong tuned baselines justify complexity.

Do not infer a universal leaderboard.

CTR improvements are often tiny in absolute AUC/NE but commercially meaningful. Public datasets, negative sampling, embedding size, regularization, and hyperparameter budgets can reorder the models.

Foundation · 2010

Factorization Machines

Instead of learning an independent weight for every feature pair, FM gives each feature a latent vector. Two features interact through the dot product of their vectors.

explicit pairwisesparse-friendlylinear-time trick

Intuition

Suppose “user = Ana” and “item = trail shoe” never appeared together in training. A polynomial model cannot estimate their specific pair coefficient. FM can: Ana’s embedding and the shoe’s embedding were each learned from other interactions, so their dot product transfers information to the unseen pair.

Complete FM forward pass

1
\[y^{(1)}=w_0+\sum_i w_ix_i\]
First-order path.One scalar weight per raw coordinate. For sparse one-hot inputs, only active IDs contribute.
2
\[w_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=\sum_{f=1}^{k}v_{i,f}v_{j,f}\]
Factorize pair coefficients.Instead of an independent \(w_{ij}\), each feature owns \(k\) latent factors. Pair parameters share the rows \(v_i\) and \(v_j\).
3
\[y^{(2)}=\sum_{i<j}\langle v_i,v_j\rangle x_ix_j\]
Pairwise path.\(x_ix_j\) switches the pair on and also scales interactions for real-valued inputs.
4
\[z=y^{(1)}+y^{(2)},\qquad p=\sigma(z)\]
Task head.FM produces a raw score. CTR uses a sigmoid; regression can use \(z\) directly; ranking can optimize a pairwise loss.
5
\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal L_{BCE}={}&-y\log p\\&-(1-y)\log(1-p)\end{aligned}\]
End-to-end learning.The loss updates the bias, linear weights, and all active latent vectors.

Why sparsity still gives useful gradients

For active feature \(i\), \(\frac{\partial \hat y}{\partial v_{i,f}}=x_i\left(\sum_jv_{j,f}x_j-v_{i,f}x_i\right)\). Thus \(v_i\) is updated whenever feature \(i\) co-occurs with any active feature, not only when one particular pair \((i,j)\) repeats.

Second-order FM
\[\hat y(\mathbf{x})=w_0+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_ix_i+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\langle \mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\rangle x_ix_j\]

Here \(\mathbf v_i\in\mathbb R^k\). Every pair coefficient is generated from the rank-\(k\) Gram factorization \(VV^T\): \(w_{ij}=\langle\mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\rangle\) for \(i\ne j\). FM omits the diagonal self-interaction terms. Shared factor rows provide generalization and efficiency.

The computational trick

Avoid the pair loop
\[\sum_{i<j}\langle\mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\rangle x_ix_j=\frac12\sum_{f=1}^{k}\left[\left(\sum_i v_{i,f}x_i\right)^2-\sum_i v_{i,f}^2x_i^2\right]\]

The apparent \(O(n^2k)\) sum becomes \(O(nk)\). For one-hot multi-field data, only active entries need processing.

A worked forward pass

Take three active binary features with two-dimensional embeddings: \(v_u=[1,.5]\), \(v_i=[.2,1]\), and \(v_c=[-.4,.3]\). Their interaction contribution is the sum of three dot products: \(0.7 + (-0.25) + 0.22 = 0.67\). The linear terms and bias are then added before the task link function, typically a sigmoid for CTR.

FM subsumes matrix factorization — a favorite interview connection. Restrict the input to exactly two one-hot fields, user ID and item ID. Then only one pair is ever active and the model reduces to \(\hat y=w_0+w_u+w_i+\langle\mathbf v_u,\mathbf v_i\rangle\): biased matrix factorization, recovered as a special case (Rendle shows the same for SVD++ and pairwise tensor factorization). The reason FM is more general is purely the input encoding: adding context fields to \(x\) adds their pairwise terms without changing the model class. This is why FM is best understood as “MF generalized to arbitrary feature vectors,” not as a separate model family.

Training and regularization

Optimize binary cross-entropy for clicks or a ranking loss when the serving objective is pairwise/listwise. Regularize linear weights and embeddings separately; rare IDs often need stronger shrinkage. Hashing controls vocabulary but creates collisions. Numerical features can enter directly, be bucketized, or scale an embedding; these choices change what a “pair” means.

Strengths

  • Works well when pair observations are sparse.
  • Explicit pairwise inductive bias and compact parameters.
  • Efficient baseline; embeddings are reusable.

Limits

  • Standard FM stops at order two.
  • All pairs use the same latent space.
  • Dot products are a restricted interaction function.

Check your intuition

Why can FM score an unseen user-item pair?

Because it does not store a unique parameter for that pair. It composes the score from two feature vectors learned across all observations containing the user or item.

Memorize + generalize · 2016

Wide & Deep

A jointly trained linear “wide” model remembers precise co-occurrences; an embedding-fed DNN generalizes to combinations not explicitly seen.

hybridmanual crossesjoint training

Two complementary failure modes

Wide: memorization

A feature such as AND(user_installed_app=A, candidate_app=B) gets its own weight. Excellent for frequent, meaningful rules; zero transfer to a new combination.

Deep: generalization

Dense embeddings place related items/users nearby. This transfers evidence, but can over-generalize and recommend merely similar rather than truly relevant items.

Complete Wide & Deep forward pass

1
\[\phi_k(\mathbf x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_i^{c_{ki}},\quad c_{ki}\in\{0,1\}\]
Construct wide crosses.The mask \(c_k\) states which source features participate. With binary indicators, the product is one only when the whole conjunction is present.
2
\[z_{wide}=\mathbf w_w^\top[\mathbf x;\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf x)]\]
Memorization branch.A sparse linear model learns a direct coefficient for each raw or crossed feature.
3
\[\mathbf a^{(0)}=[\mathbf e_1;\ldots;\mathbf e_m]\]
Build the deep input.Categorical features look up embeddings; continuous features are normalized and concatenated.
4
\[\mathbf a^{(l+1)}=f(\mathbf W^{(l)}\mathbf a^{(l)}+\mathbf b^{(l)})\]
Generalization branch.The MLP learns a distributed nonlinear representation. It is not given a symbolic inventory of crosses.
5
\[p=\sigma(z_{wide}+\mathbf w_d^\top\mathbf a^{(L)}+b)\]
Joint output.The logits add before the sigmoid, so both branches explain the same label and train from one loss.

What joint training means

For BCE, \(\partial\mathcal L/\partial z=p-y\). That same residual multiplies the wide features, \([x;\phi(x)]\), and backpropagates through the deep tower. The branches do not train independently and average probabilities afterward.

Joint prediction
\[P(Y=1\mid\mathbf x)=\sigma\!\left(\mathbf w_{wide}^{\top}[\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf x)]+\mathbf w_{deep}^{\top}\mathbf a^{(L)}+b\right)\]

\(\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf x)\) contains engineered cross-product transformations. The deep representation follows \(\mathbf a^{(l+1)}=f(\mathbf W^{(l)}\mathbf a^{(l)}+\mathbf b^{(l)})\). Both branches receive gradients from the same loss.

How the wide crosses are built

A cross-product transform is often a conjunction over categorical strings, then one-hot encoded or feature-hashed. For example, country_US × query_shoe is active only when both inputs match. Crosses should be selected from domain hypotheses, feature-importance mining, or controlled search; indiscriminately crossing high-cardinality IDs creates huge sparse vocabularies and brittle memorization.

Train/serve skewThe exact same cross construction and vocabulary mapping must run online.
Cold startUnseen crosses fall back to zero; the deep branch carries the prediction.
Wide inference\[O(\operatorname{nnz}([x;\phi(x)]))\]

Only active raw and crossed sparse features need to be scored.

Dense-layer parameters\[\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}(h_lh_{l+1}+h_{l+1})\]

Embedding tables are additional and often dominate total parameter count.

The “wide” part is not simply a shallow neural layer.

Its distinctive value is access to deliberately constructed cross features. Without those crosses, it is only an additive linear model.

Strengths

  • Balances exact rules and embedding generalization.
  • Wide weights are inspectable.
  • Established production framing.

Limits

  • Manual cross selection can be expensive.
  • Large cross vocabularies increase memory.
  • Deep interactions remain implicit.

Interview check

Why add wide and deep logits before the sigmoid rather than average two independently calibrated probabilities?

The model is jointly trained as one generalized linear predictor. Adding logits lets each branch contribute evidence on the log-odds scale, and one BCE residual trains both branches. Averaging separately trained probabilities is a different ensemble with different optimization and calibration behavior.

FM + neural · 2017

DeepFM

DeepFM replaces Wide & Deep’s hand-built cross branch with an FM. The FM captures low-order interactions; an MLP learns higher-order patterns; both reuse the same feature embeddings.

shared embeddingspairwise + implicit high-orderend-to-end

The key economy

Each active categorical feature retrieves one embedding \(\mathbf v_i\). The FM branch uses dot products \(\langle\mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\rangle\); the deep branch concatenates those same vectors. No separate embedding table is needed, and both objectives shape the representation.

Complete DeepFM forward pass

1
\[\mathbf e_j=\mathbf V_j\mathbf x_j\in\mathbb R^k\]
Field embedding.For one-hot field \(j\), this selects one row. All fields use the same dimension \(k\), making both dot products and concatenation well-defined.
2
\[y_{FM}=w_0+\langle w,x\rangle+\sum_{j_1<j_2}\langle e_{j_1},e_{j_2}\rangle\]
Explicit low-order branch.The bias and addition unit model zero- and first-order effects; inner-product units model every field pair.
3
\[a^{(0)}=[e_1;e_2;\ldots;e_m]\]
Reuse the same embeddings.No FM pretraining and no second embedding table.
4
\[a^{(l+1)}=\sigma_l(W^{(l)}a^{(l)}+b^{(l)})\]\[y_{DNN}=w_o^Ta^{(L)}+b_o\]
Implicit nonlinear branch.Hidden units mix embedding coordinates across fields, then the final hidden state is projected to one logit.
5
\[\hat y=\sigma(y_{FM}+y_{DNN})\]
Combine at logit level.Both branches and the shared embeddings are optimized simultaneously.

Shared-parameter gradient

For field embedding \(e_j\), \(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial e_j}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial y_{FM}}\frac{\partial y_{FM}}{\partial e_j}+\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial y_{DNN}}\frac{\partial y_{DNN}}{\partial e_j}\). The learned vector is a compromise between useful pair dot products and useful DNN features.

Prediction decomposition
\[\hat y=\sigma\!\left(y_{FM}+y_{DNN}\right),\qquad y_{FM}=w_0+\sum_iw_ix_i+\sum_{i<j}\langle\mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\rangle x_ix_j\]

Why the MLP can express higher order, but not explicitly

After concatenation, hidden units mix coordinates from many fields; successive nonlinear layers can approximate functions that depend jointly on several fields. There is no dedicated node corresponding to “user × item × hour,” however, and ReLU networks may need considerable width to approximate multiplication. This is the motivation behind CIN and DCN-style product operators.

DeepFM vs Wide & Deep

Wide & Deep

Wide branch uses hand-designed cross-product features. Deep and wide input design can differ.

DeepFM

FM automatically enumerates factorized second-order interactions from raw sparse features and shares embeddings with the DNN.

Strengths

  • Little manual feature crossing.
  • Efficient shared representation.
  • Strong, understandable hybrid baseline.

Limits

  • DNN higher-order crosses are implicit.
  • FM branch is only second order.
  • MLP may inefficiently learn multiplicative structure.

Check your intuition

If the FM and DNN share embeddings, can their learned roles be cleanly separated?

Not completely. The same vectors are optimized by gradients from both branches. The architectural contributions separate at the logits, but the underlying representation is jointly shaped.

Explicit vector-wise depth · 2018

xDeepFM (requested as “DeepFMx”)

xDeepFM adds a Compressed Interaction Network (CIN): it constructs bounded-degree interactions explicitly at the level of whole field embeddings, alongside an MLP for implicit interactions.

CINvector-wise crossesexplicit + implicit
Naming clarification

The canonical paper/model is xDeepFM, short for eXtreme Deep Factorization Machine. “DeepFMx” does not identify a standard model in the primary literature and is treated here as xDeepFM.

Why “vector-wise” matters

An MLP flattens all embedding coordinates and mixes them bit by bit. CIN preserves embedding dimension while it crosses feature maps. For each embedding coordinate, it forms outer products between original fields and the previous layer’s feature maps, then compresses them with learned filters.

Complete CIN computation

1
\[X^{(0)}=[e_1,\ldots,e_m]\in\mathbb R^{m\times D},\quad H_0=m\]
Preserve field rows.\(X^{(0)}_{j,*}\) is the whole \(D\)-dimensional embedding for field \(j\).
2
\[Z^{(k)}_{i,j,*}=X^{(k-1)}_{i,*}\odot X^{(0)}_{j,*}\]
Make the interaction tensor.Every prior feature map \(i\) is multiplied coordinate-wise with every original field \(j\). Shape: \(H_{k-1}\times m\times D\).
3
\[X^{(k)}_{h,*}=\sum_{i=1}^{H_{k-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}W^{(k,h)}_{ij}Z^{(k)}_{i,j,*}\]
Compress pair maps.Each learned filter \(W^{(k,h)}\in\mathbb R^{H_{k-1}\times m}\) creates one output map without mixing embedding coordinates with one another.
4
\[p_h^{(k)}=\sum_{d=1}^{D}X^{(k)}_{h,d},\quad p^+= [p^{(1)};\ldots;p^{(L)}]\]
Pool and collect layers.Sum-pooling turns each vector map into a scalar; concatenating layers exposes multiple explicit orders to the output.
5
\[z=w_{lin}^Tx+w_{CIN}^Tp^++w_{DNN}^Ta^{(L)}+b,\quad \hat y=\sigma(z)\]
Three-way merge.First-order, explicit CIN, and implicit DNN contributions meet at the final logit.
Layer-1 example\[X^{(1)}_{h,*}=\sum_{i,j}W^{(1,h)}_{ij}(e_i\odot e_j)\]
Layer-2 example\[X^{(2)}_{h,*}=\sum_{i,j}W^{(2,h)}_{ij}(X^{(1)}_{i,*}\odot e_j)\]
Why this is called vector-wise: the same scalar filter coefficient \(W_{ij}^{(k,h)}\) multiplies all \(D\) coordinates of a field-vector product. A standard dense layer instead assigns unrelated weights to individual coordinates after flattening.

Two readings of the same layer, and why it is “compressed”

  • As an RNN: each layer's output depends on the previous hidden state \(X^{(k-1)}\) and a fixed additional input \(X^{(0)}\) that is re-injected at every step — like a recurrent cell whose “new token” is always the original field matrix. That re-injection is exactly what raises the interaction degree by one per layer.
  • As a CNN: the paper treats the intermediate tensor \(Z^{(k)}\in\mathbb R^{H_{k-1}\times m\times D}\) as an image and each \(W^{(k,h)}\in\mathbb R^{H_{k-1}\times m}\) as a filter slid along the embedding dimension \(D\); each output row \(X^{(k)}_{h,*}\) is then a feature map.
  • “Compressed”: without the filters, layer \(k\) would carry \(H_{k-1}\times m\) interaction vectors. The learned filters compress that space down to \(H_k\) feature maps, which is what keeps the network from growing exponentially with degree.
  • Cost control: the paper additionally proposes an \(L\)-order decomposition \(W^{k,h}=U^{k,h}(V^{k,h})^\top\) with \(L\ll H,\,m\), cutting CIN space complexity from \(O(mTH^2)\) to \(O(mTHL+TH^2L)\); time for a \(T\)-layer CIN is \(O(mH^2DT)\), which usually exceeds the paired DNN's cost.
CIN layer, coordinate h
\[\mathbf X^{(k)}_{h,*}=\sum_{i=1}^{H_{k-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}W^{(k,h)}_{ij}\left(\mathbf X^{(k-1)}_{i,*}\odot\mathbf X^{(0)}_{j,*}\right)\]

\(m\) is the number of fields, \(H_{k-1}\) the previous layer width, and \(\odot\) element-wise multiplication. Each layer crosses back with \(X^{(0)}\), so interaction degree grows with depth. Sum-pooling each feature map across embedding coordinates produces the CIN output.

What “explicit” buys, and what it does not

CIN’s layer index tells you the maximum degree and its maps stay vector-wise, so the interaction mechanism is explicit. But each map is still a learned weighted mixture of many field combinations. Inspecting a map is not the same as reading one symbolic rule. Split-half CIN variants reduce computation by sending only part of each layer deeper while pooling the rest for output.

Three branches

Linear

First-order feature contribution.

CIN

Explicit, bounded-degree, vector-wise interactions.

DNN

Implicit, bit-wise nonlinear interactions.

Strengths

  • Explicit high-order structure without manual crosses.
  • Preserves field/embedding organization.
  • Combines complementary explicit and implicit paths.

Limits

  • CIN cost depends on fields and layer widths.
  • More architectural knobs than DeepFM.
  • “Explicit” does not make every learned pattern human-readable.

Interview check

What does CIN preserve that a standard MLP discards immediately?

CIN preserves each interaction as a D-dimensional vector and uses the same scalar filter coefficient across its D coordinates. A standard MLP flattens all field embeddings and mixes individual coordinates with unrelated weights, so field-vector structure is no longer explicit.

Explicit bounded degree · 2017

Deep & Cross Network v1

A cross layer multiplies the original input by a learned scalar summary of the current representation. Stacking layers grows polynomial degree while adding only one vector of weights per layer.

cross networkrank-1 transformO(Ld)
Original cross layer
\[\mathbf x_{l+1}=\mathbf x_0(\mathbf w_l^{\top}\mathbf x_l)+\mathbf b_l+\mathbf x_l\]

\(\mathbf w_l^T\mathbf x_l\) is a scalar. Multiplying it by \(\mathbf x_0\) creates multiplicative terms; the residual \(\mathbf x_l\) carries lower-order terms forward. An \(L\)-layer cross network contains interactions up to degree \(L+1\).

Cross-layer calculation

1
\[s_l=\mathbf w_l^\top\mathbf x_l\in\mathbb R\]
Project the current state to one scalar.This costs \(O(d)\) and is the source of v1's coefficient-sharing constraint.
2
\[\mathbf c_l=\mathbf x_0s_l\]
Cross with the original input.Every coordinate of \(x_0\) receives the same scalar, producing products between each original coordinate and the weighted current state.
3
\[\mathbf x_{l+1}=\mathbf c_l+\mathbf b_l+\mathbf x_l\]
Add bias and residual.The residual preserves all terms already present in \(x_l\) and improves gradient flow.
4
\[\frac{\partial\mathbf x_{l+1}}{\partial\mathbf x_l}=\mathbf x_0\mathbf w_l^\top+\mathbf I\]
Backward-path structure.The learned part of the Jacobian is rank one; the identity term provides the direct residual gradient.

A two-feature expansion

Ignore bias and let \(x_0=[a,b]^T\). After one layer, the new terms include \(w_1a^2\), \((w_1+w_2)ab\), and \(w_2b^2\), plus the residual \(a,b\). The key constraint is visible: coefficients are tied through the same weight vector, not independently learned for every monomial.

Why the coefficients are restricted

In v1, \(w_l^Tx_l\) is one scalar broadcast across all coordinates of \(x_0\). The resulting polynomial coefficient tensors have special low-rank structure: DCN does not independently parameterize every pair or triple. Parameter count is only \(O(Ld)\), which explains both its efficiency and why a shallow v1 cross tower can underfit heterogeneous field relationships.

The sharpest statement of the limitation (proved in the xDeepFM paper, Theorem 2.1): if the bias terms are dropped, every hidden state of the cross network is a scalar multiple of \(x_0\). By induction, \(\mathbf x_{k}=\alpha^{k}\,\mathbf x_0\) with \(\alpha^{1}=\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf w_1+1\) and \(\alpha^{k}=\alpha^{k-1}\left(\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf w_{k}+1\right)\): the whole tower collapses to one input-dependent scalar times \(x_0\). This does not mean \(x_k\) is linear in \(x_0\) — the scalar \(\alpha^k\) itself depends on \(x_0\), which is where the polynomial terms live — but it does mean the cross tower can never rotate the representation away from the direction of \(x_0\). The deep tower and the v2 matrix cross exist precisely to escape that single direction.

Interview check

Why does adding more DCN v1 layers increase interaction degree but not remove the rank-one restriction inside each layer?

Every layer multiplies its current polynomial representation by the degree-one original input, increasing maximum degree by one. But the current state is still reduced through one scalar projection \(w_l^Tx_l\), so all coordinates share that layer's scalar gate.

More expressive crosses · 2020

DCN v2

DCN v2 replaces v1’s vector/scalar cross with a matrix transformation, allowing each output coordinate to receive a different learned mixture before multiplying by the original input.

DCN v2 cross layer

Compare v1: \(\mathbf x_0(\mathbf w_l^T\mathbf x_l)\) scales every coordinate with one scalar. V2 computes a vector \(\mathbf W_l\mathbf x_l\), then applies element-wise multiplication with \(\mathbf x_0\). This is substantially more expressive.

V1 is exactly a rank-one special case of v2. Set \(\mathbf W_l=\mathbf 1\,\mathbf w_l^\top\) (every row equal to \(w_l^\top\)). Then \(\mathbf W_l\mathbf x_l=\mathbf 1\,(\mathbf w_l^\top\mathbf x_l)\) is the v1 scalar copied into every coordinate, and \(\mathbf x_0\odot\mathbf 1(\mathbf w_l^\top\mathbf x_l)=\mathbf x_0(\mathbf w_l^\top\mathbf x_l)\) recovers the v1 layer. What v2 buys is the freedom for different rows of \(W_l\) to compute different mixtures of \(x_l\), so each coordinate of \(x_0\) can be gated by its own learned signal instead of one shared scalar. This also explains why the low-rank variant needs \(r\ge 1\) to match v1 and more rank to exceed it.

Each low-rank expert computes its own cross transformation; a gating network maps the current input to normalized expert weights and forms their weighted sum. Attention over fields · 2019

AutoInt

Treat each feature field as a token. Multi-head self-attention lets every field select and combine other fields, producing instance-dependent interaction representations.

Inside one interacting layer

Numerical fields and missingness

The AutoInt paper embeds numerical field \(i\) by scaling a learned field vector with its value; categorical fields select an embedding. In practice, normalize heavy-tailed numbers, give missingness its own indicator, and avoid treating “missing = 0” as a real zero. Attention without a mask will otherwise route through placeholder fields.

How stacked attention layers construct higher orders

One interacting layer only ever mixes the current field vectors, so where do third- and fourth-order interactions come from? The paper's own analysis (Section 4.7) runs on two mechanisms working together:

  • Layer 1 manufactures pairs. With fields \(x_1,\ldots,x_4\), the first layer encodes second-order combinations such as \(g(x_1,x_2)\) and \(g(x_3,x_4)\) into the updated field vectors, with the ReLU supplying the non-additivity.
  • Residuals keep the raw ingredients available. Because \(e_m^{Res}\) still contains the original \(e_m\), a second layer can combine the pair-bearing vector \(e_1^{Res}\) with the raw \(x_3\) inside \(e_3^{Res}\) to reach third order.
  • Combined representations combine. The same second layer can also attend \(e_1^{Res}\) (carrying \(g(x_1,x_2)\)) onto \(e_3^{Res}\) (carrying \(g(x_3,x_4)\)), reaching fourth order in only two layers. The maximum reachable order therefore grows exponentially with depth, not linearly — a few interacting layers suffice for high-order patterns.

Contrast this with DCN, where each layer multiplies by the degree-one input and the maximum degree grows by exactly one per layer. AutoInt trades that precise degree bookkeeping for input-dependent routing: which combinations actually form is decided per example by the attention weights, not fixed by the architecture.

Attention weights are useful diagnostics, not guaranteed explanations.

A large attention coefficient means a value vector was strongly routed at that layer/head. Residual paths, later layers, value transformations, and the output weights still determine the prediction.

AutoInt vs a standard Transformer

The core operator is multi-head self-attention, but the objects are heterogeneous feature fields rather than a word sequence. There is usually no causal mask or meaningful positional order. Numerical fields are projected into the same embedding dimension; categorical fields use embedding lookup.

Strengths

  • Input-dependent field interactions.
  • Multiple heads capture different relations.
  • Preserves field structure and supports inspection.

Limits

  • Pairwise attention cost is quadratic in field count.
  • Interpretability claims need care.
  • Deep stacks require stabilization/tuning.

Interview check

AutoInt contains no explicit product operator like FM's \(\langle v_i,v_j\rangle x_ix_j\). Why can two interacting layers still capture a fourth-order interaction such as \(g(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\)?

Layer one encodes second-order combinations like \(g(x_1,x_2)\) and \(g(x_3,x_4)\) into the updated field vectors, with ReLU providing non-additivity. Residual connections preserve those combined representations, so layer two can attend \(e_1^{Res}\) onto \(e_3^{Res}\) and merge two second-order features into one fourth-order feature. Maximum order therefore grows exponentially with depth. The trade-off versus FM/DCN is that the interaction is a learned, input-dependent composition rather than a fixed algebraic product with a readable degree.

Heterogeneous ensemble · 2022

DHEN

Deep Hierarchical Ensemble Network treats interaction modules as complementary experts. Each layer runs several operators in parallel, combines their outputs, and feeds the enriched representation to the next layer.

The motivating observation

MLP, cross, attention, and product modules may all claim high-order interactions, yet perform differently across datasets. “Order” alone does not describe their inductive biases: their parameter tying, field preservation, nonlinearities, and routing differ. DHEN keeps multiple biases.

Operator choice is an ablation question

Adding every module is not automatically optimal. Redundant experts waste compute, very wide concatenation increases activation memory, and module outputs can have incompatible scales. Normalize/project before fusion, ablate modules under equal compute, and distinguish gains from parameter count versus heterogeneous inductive bias.

Why systems work is part of the contribution

A deeper, wider ensemble increases communication, memory traffic, and synchronization. DHEN co-designs distributed training: hybrid sharding of large embeddings, pipelining/overlap, and optimized interaction modules are necessary for the architecture to be practical at Meta-scale workloads.

Reported result, interpreted correctly

The paper reports a 0.27% improvement in normalized entropy and 1.2× training throughput versus its stated state-of-the-art baseline on its large-scale setting. These are paper-specific results, not a guarantee on another dataset or stack.

Wukong

Wukong asks a newer question: can recommendation quality keep improving predictably as feature-interaction compute grows? Its answer is a deep stack built around factorization-machine-style interaction layers and compression.

Wukong does not flatten all fields into one \(nd\)-vector. It keeps a matrix whose rows are whole embeddings. This distinction matters because the FM block computes embedding-wise interactions: one dot product per pair of rows.

1. Exact interaction-stack layer

FMB and LCB consume the same \(X_i\) in parallel. FMB manufactures new interaction representations. LCB only linearly recombines existing rows. Concatenation joins their roles; a residual protects existing information; LayerNorm stabilizes the next layer.

Forward pass through layer i

1

Interaction branch.Computes pairwise dot-product interactions and transforms them back into \(n_F\) embedding rows.

2. Factorization Machine Block (FMB)

The FMB has two conceptually separate jobs. The FM operator creates explicit interactions; the following MLP translates the interaction matrix into a fixed number of dense embedding rows that another FMB can consume.

Inside FMB at layer i

1
\[\mathbf S_i=\operatorname{FM}(\mathbf X_i)=\mathbf X_i\mathbf X_i^\top\in\mathbb R^{n_i\times n_i}\]
All row-pair dot products.Entry \((p,q)\) is \(S_{pq}=\mathbf x_{i,p}^\top\mathbf x_{i,q}=\sum_{r=1}^{d}X_{i,pr}X_{i,qr}\). Off-diagonal entries encode pair interactions; the diagonal contains self-interactions/norms.
2
\[\mathbf s_i=\operatorname{LN}(\operatorname{flatten}(\mathbf S_i))\]
Vectorize and normalize.The \(n_i\times n_i\) interaction matrix becomes a vector. Normalization prevents its scale from drifting as row norms and layer depth change.
3

Learn interaction mixtures.The MLP is not relied on to discover multiplication; FM already performed it. The MLP selects and remixes pair signals into \(n_Fd\) coordinates.

Return to embedding form.The next stack layer again sees a set of \(d\)-dimensional rows, so the same FMB design can be repeated.
Why the order can double\[o(\mathbf x_p^\top\mathbf x_q)=o(\mathbf x_p)+o(\mathbf x_q)\]

If both input rows contain terms of maximum order \(2^{i-1}\), their dot product can contain order \(2^i\).

Concrete two-layer expansion: let \(X_1\) contain first-order rows \(a,b\) and second-order row \(ab\). Materializing \(XX^T\) is quadratic in the number of rows. Wukong exploits the fact that \(\operatorname{rank}(XX^T)\le d\), typically with \(d\ll n\), and asks only for a learned \(n\times k\) projection of that matrix.

4. Linear Compression Block (LCB)

LCB definition
\[\operatorname{LCB}_i(\mathbf X_i)=\mathbf W_i^L\mathbf X_i,\qquad \mathbf W_i^L\in\mathbb R^{n_L\times n_i}\]

Each output row is \(\ell_r=\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}W^L_{rj}x_{i,j}\). This changes the basis and row count but performs no products, so it does not increase interaction order. It has three jobs:

Keep low-order signal

Without LCB/residual, a deep stack could overemphasize newly created high-order terms and lose direct access to simpler predictors.

Control width

\(n_L\) fixes how many existing-information rows survive, preventing row counts from growing with every layer.

Learn a new basis

The next FMB interacts useful mixtures of prior rows, not only the original field axes.

LCB is not the compression inside the optimized FM.

They are separate. The optimized FM uses \(Y\) to avoid an \(n\times n\) interaction matrix inside FMB. LCB uses \(W^L\) as the parallel non-interacting branch of the stack layer.

Why residual + LayerNorm matter

Dot products and MLPs repeatedly change scale. The residual creates a short identity path for activations and gradients; LayerNorm controls per-example representation scale. If row counts differ, the residual projection is learned rather than silently padding/truncating.

Interview drill

Why not just stack ordinary FMs?

A classical FM produces a scalar score. Wukong’s FMB transforms the pair matrix into new embedding rows, making its output a valid input to another FM block.

Why both FMB and LCB?

FMB increases interaction order; LCB preserves/compresses existing orders. Together they avoid forcing all useful signal through repeated multiplication.

Why can order reach 2ᴸ?

At layer i, FMB can pair two representations each containing order \(2^{i-1}\); multiplication adds their orders. LCB/residual keep the lower orders.

What is the main bottleneck?

Naive \(XX^T\) is quadratic in row count. Optimized FM uses \(X(X^TY)\); FMB MLP and distributed embedding traffic may then become dominant.

How is it unlike AutoInt?

AutoInt routes value vectors with normalized, input-dependent attention. Wukong explicitly computes embedding dot-product interactions and uses MLPs after multiplication.

What would you ablate?

Remove FMB, LCB, residual, optimized projection, or vary L/nF/nL/k under matched compute. Track log loss plus throughput and memory, not quality alone.

Scaling claim

Across six public datasets, Wukong reports quality advantages over tuned baselines. The scaling evidence comes from an internal Meta dataset the paper describes as 146 billion examples with 720 features — roughly 30× more samples and 20× more features than Criteo Terabyte. There the authors report that quality follows the scaling trend with compute across about two orders of magnitude, up to and beyond 100 GFLOP per example, with depth \(L\) scaled first (to raise reachable order) and width-type knobs (\(n_F,n_L,k\), MLP size) scaled afterward.

A scaling law is an empirical regularity, not an architectural theorem.

It depends on the dataset, model family, optimizer, allocation among \(L,n_F,n_L,k,h,d\), and observed compute range. Extrapolation to another recommender must be re-measured.

  • Simple width/depth upscaling story.
  • Explicit focus on sustained gains with compute.

Limits

  • Newest model here; less independent production evidence.
  • Large variants require substantial infrastructure.
  • Scaling observations may not transfer universally.

Input-dependent attention among fields versus a fixed algebraic polynomial cross operator over the flattened input.

DHEN vs one best module

DHEN assumes interaction operators contain non-overlapping signal and explicitly preserves several inductive biases.

Wukong vs shallow FM

A shallow FM stops at pairs; Wukong repeatedly applies FM-style blocks to interaction representations, raising order with depth.
